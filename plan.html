Part 4 · Plan

Plan for the next three weeks

Our goals: get cleaner measurements, test one room factor on its own, connect the simulation to real room geometry, and let visitors hear the results.

  1. Week 1 · Nov 13 – Nov 19

    Task 1: Re-measure both rooms with a sine sweep

    Generate a 5-s exponential sine sweep from 100 Hz to 16 kHz and its inverse filter (Farina's method). Play it in both rooms at the same eight positions, with the recorder on a stand, a fixed-gain recording app, a sync click, and three takes per position. Convolve each recording with the inverse filter to get \(h[n]\), then repeat our EDT / \(T_{20}\) / \(C_{50}\) analysis.

    Done when: peak-to-noise ratio of at least 35 dB, so \(T_{20}\) can be reported, and the three takes agree within 0.02 s.

    Lead: Yongjia (recording) · Yingqian (analysis)

  2. Week 2 · Nov 20 – Nov 26

    Task 2: Controlled material test

    In the bathroom, keep everything fixed (positions, phone, level) and record twice: bare walls, then with towels and blankets covering the walls and tub. Compare EDT, \(T_{20}\) and octave-band \(C_{50}\). This is the only way to separate the effect of absorbing material from room size and placement.

    Done when: we can say how much, and at which frequencies, soft material shortens the decay, with error bars from repeated takes.

    Lead: Yongjia · Yingqian

  3. Week 2 · Nov 20 – Nov 26 (in parallel)

    Task 3: Image-source model of the two rooms

    Write an image-source simulation for a rectangular room using the measured dimensions, source and recorder positions. It places a mirror-image source behind each wall, giving the delay and strength of every early reflection. Add an exponential tail with the measured \(T_{60}\). Compare the simulated and measured impulse responses and their energy-decay curves.

    Done when: simulated first-reflection times match the measured ones within a few ms, or we can explain why they do not (furniture, unmeasured distances).

    Lead: Yiheng

  4. Week 3 · Nov 27 – Dec 3

    Task 4: Auralization and interactive website demo

    Convolve dry speech and music with the measured and modeled impulse responses so visitors can hear "the same voice in our bedroom vs. our bathroom". Add a browser demo (Web Audio) with sliders for reflection delay, reflection strength and \(T_{60}\), based on our simple model. Write the final results and limitations, and finish the website.

    Done when: the website has A/B listening for every room and model, and the final write-up is complete.

    Lead: Yingqian (website) · Yiheng (demo) · everyone reviews

If something goes wrong

  • The phone still compresses the sweep: borrow a USB microphone or a handheld recorder with manual gain, or lower the playback level until two levels give the same response.
  • No time for the material test: prioritize the sine-sweep re-measurement, since better data improves every other result.
  • Image-source model too far from the measurements: keep it as a lower bound for the early reflections and explain the gap (furniture, unmeasured distances).
