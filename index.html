How does a room change a sound?

Clap your hands in a bathroom and in a bedroom and they sound different, even though the clap is the same. We treat each room as a filter, describe that filter by its impulse response, and measure it in two real rooms.

Yiheng Li · Yongjia Xia · Yingqian Sun

The idea in one equation

Between a sound source and a listener, a room behaves approximately like a linear time-invariant (LTI) system. What you hear, \(y[n]\), is the original sound \(x[n]\) convolved with the room's impulse response \(h[n]\):

\[ y[n] = x[n] * h[n] \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Y(e^{j\omega}) = X(e^{j\omega})\,H(e^{j\omega}) \]

The impulse response is the room's "fingerprint". It begins with the direct sound, continues with early reflections off walls, floor, ceiling and furniture, and ends in a reverberant tail that dies away roughly exponentially. If we know \(h[n]\), we can make any dry recording sound as if it were played in that room. If we know \(x[n]\) and record \(y[n]\), we can work backwards to estimate \(h[n]\).

What we are doing

1 · SIMULATE

Build our own rooms

We design synthetic impulse responses (single echo, multiple reflections, early reflections plus a decaying tail) and convolve a clap recording with them.

2 · MEASURE

Record real rooms

A phone plays the same clap recording in a bedroom and a bathroom. A hand-held recorder captures it at four positions in each room.

3 · ESTIMATE & COMPARE

Recover each room's \(h[n]\)

Regularized deconvolution gives an estimated impulse response, from which we compute decay time, clarity and spectra and compare the rooms.

  • Convolution reproduces exactly the echoes we design, and an echo in time shows up as a comb-shaped ripple in frequency.
  • The small, hard-surfaced bathroom decays more slowly at first and is less "clear" than the larger, furnished bedroom, at all eight positions. The two rooms differ in several ways at once, so we treat this as an observation, not a proven cause.
  • Much of what we measure comes from the phones themselves: their loudspeaker shapes the spectrum, and what looks like automatic gain control breaks the LTI assumption. Fixing this is the main goal for the next three weeks.
